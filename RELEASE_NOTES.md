Multiclassing
- Characters can have more than one class. Details > Identity has a Classes table with each class's subclass, level, hit die, hit dice left, caster type and spellcasting ability. Your total level sets your proficiency bonus.
- Level up from Details or the Play tab: pick a class you already have or a new one. Hearthsheet adds the level, takes the average HP or your roll, adds the new class's multiclass armor, weapon and tool proficiencies, and lists what's left to do by hand, such as skill choices and class features. A missing ability score requirement shows as a warning and never blocks you.
- Spell slots follow the multiclass table for full, half and third casters. Warlock Pact Magic slots can be cast on their own and come back on a short rest.
- Each spellcasting class has its own ability, save DC and spell attack. Tag a spell with its class and it shows that class's DC.
- Hit dice are kept per class. A short rest lets you choose which die to spend, and a long rest gives back the largest dice first.
- Setting a subclass to Eldritch Knight or Arcane Trickster makes the class a third caster and fills in its spell slots. Renaming a class keeps its spells attached to it.
- Blood Hunter is a built-in class, with its hit die, saving throws, multiclass requirements and proficiencies.
- The printed sheet: a single-class character looks as before. With two or more classes, the Class & Level box lists each class with your total level, and each spellcasting class gets its own line.

Upgrading your characters
- Your existing characters open as they were: their class becomes the first row of the Classes table.
- Characters saved by 1.4.0 use a newer format. If you use Hearthsheet on more than one computer, update them all: an older version won't open these characters and leaves them alone when it syncs instead of overwriting them.
