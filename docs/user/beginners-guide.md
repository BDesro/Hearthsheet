# Beginner's guide to 5e

New to Dungeons & Dragons? This page explains, briefly and in plain words, what the numbers on your character sheet are for, and where each one shows up in Hearthsheet. It doesn't teach the rules themselves. For those, see [where to look things up](#where-to-look-things-up).

## Ability scores and modifiers

Every character has six abilities: Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom and Charisma. Each has a **score**, usually between 8 and 20, and a **modifier** worked out from it. A score of 10 or 11 gives +0, and every two points above or below that moves the modifier by one. You'll add the modifier to dice rolls far more often than you use the score itself.

In Hearthsheet you type the scores in **Details → Ability Scores & Saves**. The modifiers are worked out for you.

## Proficiency bonus

Your **proficiency bonus** grows as your character levels up. You add it to rolls for things your character is trained in: certain skills, saving throws, weapons and tools. Hearthsheet shows it in Play under **At a glance**.

## Saving throws and skills

A **saving throw** is a roll to resist something, such as dodging a blast or shaking off a poison. A **skill** is a roll for something your character attempts, such as sneaking, persuading or climbing. Each one uses an ability modifier, plus your proficiency bonus if you're proficient. Mark your proficiencies in **Details**, and the totals appear on the sheet.

## Armor class and hit points

**Armor class (AC)** is how hard you are to hit. **Hit points (HP)** are how much harm you can take. At 0 HP you fall unconscious and start making **death saves**. Your **hit dice** are a pool you can spend on a short rest to win back HP. Play tracks all of these.

## Spells

Spellcasters have **spell slots**, which are the fuel for casting, sorted by spell level. Casting a spell uses up a slot, and rests bring slots back. Your **spell save DC** is the number a target must reach to resist your spell, and your **spell attack** bonus is what you add when a spell makes an attack roll. Hearthsheet works out both, and spends slots when you **Cast**.

## Rests

A **short rest** is a breather of about an hour, when you can spend hit dice. A **long rest** is a full night's sleep, which brings back hit points, spell slots and most limited-use features. Both buttons are in Play.

## Where to look things up

Hearthsheet tracks your numbers but deliberately includes no rules or spell text. For that, keep a reference open next to it:

- **The D&D 5e wikidot** at [dnd5e.wikidot.com](https://dnd5e.wikidot.com) is a quick place to look up spells, class features, feats and other rules while you play. It's a community-run site that Hearthsheet isn't affiliated with.
- **The System Reference Document (SRD)** is the free, official core rules from Wizards of the Coast, at [dnd.wizards.com](https://dnd.wizards.com/resources/systems-reference-document).
- **Your Dungeon Master** is the final word at your table.
